SO14.I9 · EW2

Net income by activity

How much remains after the costs attributable to each activity?

Calculation and interpretation

Activity net income = activity income − attributable costs. Charts show means per activity for the illustrative six-month economic period. The headline shows the median across selected activity records. Own consumption, family labour and depreciation are not valued in this fixture; inclusion in the final indicator remains under review.

Synthetic income assumption: gold mining has substantially higher net income than the other activities. This does not make it nature-positive. The cost view includes seven collected cash-cost categories for illustration. Investment and external support are kept separate. A net income level alone does not establish profitability across different activities.

activity_income · activity_costs · net_activity_income
SO14.I10 · EW2 / EW3 / EW1

Household income by productive activity

How does each productive activity contribute to total household income?

Calculation and interpretation

Activity share = income from that productive activity / total household monetary income × 100. The chart shows the mean of household shares, with non-operators contributing zero to each activity’s mean. Activities with the same sector code are grouped for display. Amounts are mean net soles per household for the illustrative six-month period.

The denominator includes pensions, transfers and all other monetary sources, which continue to be collected. Activity shares can therefore sum to less than 100%. A change in share can reflect growth of the activity or a decline in other income. The proposed indicator name and the composition of income are in Q&A for team validation. Non-monetary benefits are recorded separately.

SO14.I11 · EW2

Stability and seasonality of household income

Three separate measures describe perceived regularity, the duration of the low season and the intensity of its income gap.

1. Perceived stability

la pregunta ENAHO de estabilidad categories, with a 12-month reference period. The category codes are not averaged.

2. Low-season duration

3. Relative income reduction

Calculation and interpretation

Synthetic seasonality assumption: July–September is the high season, so those months are not selected as low-income months. This assumption only shapes the preview; the questionnaire will record each household’s reported pattern. Duration = number of reported low-income months, from 0 to 12. Intensity = 100 × (typical high-season monthly income − typical low-season monthly income) / typical high-season monthly income, calculated for each household before summarising.

Month bars count households identifying that month as low season; they do not show monthly income amounts. Typical-month amounts use the same household income concept. Duration and intensity are proposed measures requiring field validation, not a validated ENAHO seasonality scale. Six-month participant rounds have overlapping 12-month recall windows.

SO14.I12 · EW3

Shock exposure and recovery

Follow the sequence from shock to impact, response and recovery.

Calculation and interpretation

The five views report household counts separately. Exposure and strategy responses can be multiple. Impact refers to shock-exposed households; strategies and recovery to households reporting an economic loss; expected time to those whose loss remains unresolved or partially resolved. These are display populations for complete synthetic records, not adopted denominator or missing-data rules.

el bloque ENAHO de shocks y recuperación provides the reference structure. The local shock list and questions remain under review. No shock does not mean high coping capacity. Borrowing, using savings or selling assets does not automatically mean compromising future livelihoods. The Indicators tab now proposes recovery_complete × future_income_capacity_preserved. The new capacity question and this joint rule await team validation; these charts retain the five reference components and do not simulate future-capacity results.

shock_type · shock_impact · coping_strategy · recovery_status · expected_recovery_time
SO14.I8 · EW1

Nature-positive producers

How many selected producers operate nature-positive activities?

Calculation and interpretation

Count unique producers classified as nature-positive, alongside activity-level counts. Synthetic classifications are supplied to demonstrate the display, not generated by a final operational rule. Mining and logging of natural forest are excluded; timber plantations require previously degraded land. The evidence and aggregation criteria remain under review.

Economic performance is shown separately in I9 and I7. Non-participation does not demonstrate replication of a Wyss-promoted alternative. Permanence after support ends and expansion between rounds need support histories, comparable units and agreed definitions.

SO14.I7 · EW1

Profitability and ecological compatibility

Joint state of economic performance and ecological condition in the linked subset.

Ecological evidence and interpretation

I16–I18 must be recalculated for the linked area; landscape values cannot be assigned to individual concessions. I19 camera-trap and acoustic evidence exists only where monitoring coverage is sufficient. Ecological metrics are not combined into an arbitrary score.

Fixture assumptions: one activity and one concession for each classified producer; illustrative economic and ecological flags supplied directly. Expert economic thresholds, ecological reference levels and multi-activity aggregation remain open. Baseline state needs one matched period; trajectory needs at least two comparable economic and ecological observations.

SO14.I6 · EW1

Landscape household income share

Read the share together with its economic numerator and denominator.

Calculation and interpretation

Share = 100 × Σ(w × household nature-positive income) / Σ(w × total household income). This is a ratio of totals, not a mean of household shares. All synthetic weights equal 1; the real sampling design and weights are still pending.

This household-based approximation does not represent the entire territorial economy. Filters produce a subgroup result; the full landscape sample is shown as a reference. Programme follow-up records are not pooled into the landscape estimate. Baseline establishes a level; change in percentage points requires a second comparable landscape round.

household_nature_positive_income · household_total_income · survey_weight · territorial_income_share
Additional analyses suggested by the original workbook

Continuity and expansion · I8

Compare nature-positive activity before and after support ends; distinguish verified new adopters from existing adopters; track unique producers and hectares. Support histories, areas and adoption dates need to be collected and definitions agreed.

Income origin and non-monetary value · I10

Distinguish income from standing forest, agroforestry and restored land. Describe own consumption, forest foods, water, materials and reciprocity separately. Detailed variables and coding remain to be designed.

Distribution and wellbeing · I13–I15

Potential future extension: life satisfaction (0–10), perceived livelihood choice, unsatisfied basic needs and food insecurity. These measures are documented in Indicators; this dashboard does not include simulated wellbeing results.

Change and group differences

For participant rounds, follow the same producers and households over time. With sufficient observations, disaggregate by activity, gender, age and group. No difference between participation groups can be read as a programme effect without an evaluation design.
